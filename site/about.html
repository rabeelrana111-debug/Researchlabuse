About us

About Research Lab USA

We publish reference material on research compounds for people who read the methods section first — researchers, laboratory staff, and anyone who would rather see the evidence than be told a conclusion.

Our purpose

Sourcing information about research compounds is harder than it should be. Supplier pages read like advertising, forum threads contradict each other, and the primary literature is often behind a paywall or written for specialists. What sits in between is mostly written to rank in search results rather than to be useful.

We started publishing to fill that gap: material detailed enough to act on, written plainly enough to read, and honest about the considerable amount that remains unknown.

What we are working towards

A reference that researchers reach for first, and trust because it tells them when the evidence is weak rather than only when it is strong. Every guide is built to answer three questions: what is this material, how should it be handled, and what has actually been shown about it.

An absence of evidence is a finding in its own right, and it is stated as clearly as a positive result.

How we write

Claims are tied to the study behind them, so you can read the original rather than take our word for it. Where findings come from a single research group, or have never been independently replicated, we say so — a citation count is not the same as a body of evidence.

We describe materials, not outcomes in people. Chemical identity, purity, physical form, solubility and storage are all fair game. What a compound does in a human body is not something we assert, because for almost everything catalogued here nobody reliably knows.

Corrections

When something turns out to be wrong, the page is updated with a dated note describing what changed and why. Pages are not quietly edited: if you relied on an earlier version, you should be able to see that it changed.

What you will find here

Four things we try to get right

Sourced material

Guides built from published work, with each claim linked to the study behind it so you can check the original.

Handling detail

Storage, solubility and preparation written out in full, including the steps that get left off because they seem obvious to whoever wrote them down.

Stated uncertainty

Where the record is thin, contested, or rests on one group's work, that appears at the top of the page rather than buried at the bottom.

Verification first

How to read a certificate of analysis and what batch traceability should look like — because mislabelling is well documented across this whole product category.

Why read us rather than a supplier page

A supplier has an interest in the answer. We would rather tell you that a compound is poorly characterised, that its development was discontinued, or that the enthusiasm around it outruns the published work — all of which appear on pages here.

That is the whole proposition. A reference that only ever sounds positive is not a reference.
