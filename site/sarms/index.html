Topic

Selective androgen receptor modulators

Reference material on SARMs as laboratory research compounds — identity, handling, and what the literature actually shows.

What this section covers

SARMs are a class of compounds studied for their selective activity at the androgen receptor. Our guides describe them as materials: structure, molecular weight, solubility, stability and storage.

Where we discuss research findings, we describe what was measured, in what model, and what the authors concluded — not what the result might mean for a person. Most of this literature is preclinical, and treating it otherwise misrepresents it.

None of these compounds is approved for human or veterinary use in any jurisdiction we are aware of. Several are prohibited in competitive sport. That context belongs in any honest write-up, so it appears in ours.

Compounds in this section

GW-501516 (Cardarine)

A PPARδ agonist, frequently grouped with SARMs although it acts on a different receptor family entirely.

MK-2866 (Ostarine)

One of the most extensively studied SARMs, and the one with the largest published clinical record.

RAD-140 (Testolone)

A non-steroidal SARM whose published record is preclinical, with notably less human data than MK-2866.
