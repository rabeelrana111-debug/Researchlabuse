Peptides

BPC-157

A synthetic pentadecapeptide studied in animal models, with almost no published human data.

Written by: Research Lab USA Editorial Team Published: August 20, 2026 Last updated: September 28, 2026

What it is

BPC-157 is a synthetic peptide of fifteen amino acids, derived from a sequence identified in gastric juice. It is supplied lyophilized and requires reconstitution before use.

State of the evidence

The published literature is almost entirely preclinical, and a large share of it comes from a small number of research groups. That concentration matters: findings replicated only within one group are weaker evidence than the raw publication count suggests.

Controlled human data is minimal. Claims circulating outside the literature considerably outrun what has actually been demonstrated.

Handling and stability

Store lyophilized material cold and protected from light. Reconstituted peptide is markedly less stable than the lyophilized form, and repeated freeze-thaw cycling degrades it. Record the reconstitution date — a peptide is not the same material three weeks later.

Regulatory status

Not an approved medicine. In 2023 the FDA placed it in a category of substances barred from compounding pending further evaluation.
