Adrafinil

A prodrug that metabolises to modafinil, with a correspondingly different pharmacokinetic profile.

What it is

Adrafinil is a prodrug: it is metabolised in the liver to modafinil, which is the active compound. It was developed in France in the 1970s and later withdrawn from the market there.

Why the prodrug relationship matters

Because conversion happens in the liver, the compound places a metabolic load that modafinil itself does not. Studies on modafinil do not transfer cleanly to adrafinil for that reason, and treating the two as equivalent misreads both.

Regulatory status

Not an approved medicine in the United States. Modafinil, its metabolite, is a prescription medicine and a controlled substance there — a distinction worth understanding before ordering either.

Handling

Supplied as a powder. Confirm purity and identity against the certificate of analysis for your batch.
